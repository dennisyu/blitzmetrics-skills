## Definitive articles show what they are and where they fit

- **Mark every canonical how-to hub visibly as a Definitive Article or Definitive
  SOP.** Put the marker above the opening summary so a reader can distinguish the
  maintained source of truth from a supporting post before scrolling.
- **Lead with the result, not a wall of setup.** Give the 2–3 sentence plain-language
  summary first, then a compact outcome/checklist block. Move audience explanation,
  history and secondary evidence below the visual orientation.
- **Show where the task fits in the system.** When an established framework exists,
  reuse its canonical diagram near the top and highlight the exact subcomponents this
  task uses. Keep the surrounding components visible but muted so the reader sees both
  the local path and the whole machine. Caption what the highlight means.
- **Do not redraw the framework from memory.** Reuse the maintained labels, order,
  palette and relationships. A task-specific flow diagram may follow later, but it does
  not replace the system-placement view.
- **Mark only the canonical hub.** Supporting stories, updates and meta-articles point
  to the hub; they do not wear the definitive marker themselves.

No fleet regex enforces this honestly: the crawler cannot infer whether a page is the
canonical hub, which framework governs it, or which boxes should be highlighted. Enforce
it in the definitive-article preflight and visual review.
